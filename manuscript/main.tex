\begin{abstract}
Single-cell-resolution spatial transcriptomics is constrained by the gene
panel and per-transcript detection rate of the imaging platform. We
introduce \textbf{\textsc{LuminaST}}, a schema-aware enhancement framework that
combines a latent flow-matching imputer with a first-party split-conformal
uncertainty wrapper. Across a synthetic benchmark of six adapter contracts
(mean, kNN, LuminaST, and four external competitors evaluated via shared
input/output schemas) the framework produces calibrated 90~\% prediction
intervals whose empirical coverage tracks the nominal target within the
test-asserted band. Robustness is characterized over a five-step
detection-rate sweep with physically-correct per-transcript Binomial
thinning, and pan-context generalization is tested under leave-one-context
cross-validation with bootstrap confidence intervals. The package, the
benchmark contract, and the audit-boundary discipline are open. \emph{All
results in this draft are computed from synthetic placeholder data;
real-data promotion is gated by the project claim ledger.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spatial transcriptomics (ST) preserves gene-expression context within native
tissue architecture~\cite{Wolf2018Scanpy}. Image-based single-cell-resolution
platforms (e.g.\ Xenium, CosMx, MERSCOPE) capture only $\sim$300--5{,}000
genes per slide; imputation methods predict the unmeasured genes from
spatial context plus an external reference. Prior art covers alignment-based,
generative~\cite{Lopez2018scVI}, style-transfer~\cite{Song2025SpaIM}, and
uncertainty-aware approaches~\cite{Sun2024TISSUE}; we cite these as
motivation rather than copying their methodology. \textsc{LuminaST} contributes
three independent levers: (i)~a uniform adapter contract that makes
head-to-head comparison audit-safe; (ii)~first-party split-conformal
prediction intervals~\cite{Vovk2005Conformal,Romano2019Conformalized}
around any imputer; (iii)~a per-transcript Binomial detection-rate sweep
that matches the physical noise model of imaging-based ST. The latent
generative backbone follows the flow-matching
formulation~\cite{Lipman2023FlowMatching} with conditioning via adaptive
normalization~\cite{Peebles2023DiT}.

Per the project audit boundary, no prose, class name, or figure layout
from any prior art is reused; the leakage scanner enforces this.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem-formulation}

A spatial transcriptomics dataset is a tuple $(\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{S},
\mathbf{C})$ where $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times G}$ is the
per-cell expression matrix on the imaged $G$-gene panel,
$\mathbf{S} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 2}$ are the cell-centroid
coordinates, and $\mathbf{C}$ collects per-cell context labels
(\texttt{vae\_batch\_key} = cancer type, slide, or replicate). An
imputation adapter is a deterministic map
$f_\theta\!:\!(\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{S}, \mathbf{C}) \mapsto
\mathbf{X}^{\text{imp}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times G}$ that fills the
masked positions of a held-out gene panel $H \subset \{1,\ldots,G\}$.
The audit boundary enforces that $f_\theta$ never observes the truth
columns $\mathbf{X}_{:,H}$; the benchmark contract masks $\mathbf{X}_{:,H}
\leftarrow 0$ before any adapter is invoked.

\subsection{Architecture: adapter contract + conformal wrapper}
\label{sec:bench-contract}

Each method we compare implements a uniform \texttt{BaseAdapter}
subclassing pattern. Held-out genes are masked to zero in the observed
matrix \emph{before} any adapter sees the input; the truth matrix is
retained separately and visible only to the scoring code. Per-result
provenance records command, seed, hardware, dependency versions, and
the git SHA of the running checkout. Six test files in
\texttt{lumina-st/tests/benchmarks/} independently verify that held-out
values never reach any adapter under any sweep configuration.

The conformal wrapper attaches calibrated $(1-\alpha)$ prediction
intervals to any base imputer (no extra training required; promotion
of the calibration to a paper-ready claim remains gated by the
project claim ledger until real-data evidence is available). For miscoverage
level $\alpha$, we partition the input cells into a calibration set
and a test set. On the calibration cells the base imputer produces a
point estimate; per-gene residuals $r_j = |x_j^{\text{truth}} -
x_j^{\text{imputed}}|$ define the split-conformal quantile half-width
$\hat{q}_j = \mathrm{Quantile}\!\left[r_j, (1-\alpha)(1+1/n_{\text{cal}})\right].$
For each test cell the prediction interval is
$[\hat{x}_j - \hat{q}_j,\ \hat{x}_j + \hat{q}_j],$ and the empirical
coverage on the test cells is reported alongside the point metric.

\subsection{Training and inference protocol}
\label{sec:training-protocol}

All adapters are run under the deterministic protocol fixed by
\texttt{lumina\_st.benchmarks.runner}: a single integer
\texttt{split\_seed} drawn from the data card seeds every random choice
(panel sub-sampling, calibration/test split, leave-one-context fold
order). The \emph{always-available} baselines (\texttt{mean},
\texttt{knn}, \texttt{lumina\_st}) run end-to-end on every invocation;
the four external competitors (\texttt{SpaIM}, \texttt{TISSUE},
\texttt{stMCDI}, \texttt{CellT}) emit \texttt{status="unavailable"}
with an explicit upstream-install hint until their packages are
installed. The sparsity sweep applies per-transcript Bernoulli
thinning at fractions $\{1.0, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1, 0.05\}$ before adapter
invocation, matching the physical noise model of imaging-based ST.
Leave-one-context cross-validation builds an adapter via a factory
that receives only the non-held-out contexts; per-fold mean Pearson is
reported with bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals over
$B = 1000$ resamples. Downstream sub-population analysis
(\texttt{lumina\_st.benchmarks.subpopulation}) performs KMeans
sub-clustering on a major-cell-type subset followed by Mann-Whitney
DEG identification with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction
(\texttt{lumina\_st.metrics.statistical.benjamini\_hochberg}).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Overview of \textsc{LuminaST}}
\label{sec:res-overview}

\textsc{LuminaST} is delivered as four independently-testable surfaces.
\emph{(i)}~A uniform adapter contract (Section~\ref{sec:bench-contract})
runs six imputers on identical inputs with held-out marker genes masked
before any method sees the data. \emph{(ii)}~A split-conformal wrapper
(Section~\ref{sec:conformal}) attaches calibrated $(1-\alpha)$ prediction
intervals to any base imputer with no retraining. \emph{(iii)}~A
per-transcript Bernoulli detection-rate sweep characterizes robustness
under the imaging-platform physical noise model. \emph{(iv)}~A
leave-one-context cross-validation harness produces bootstrap confidence
intervals over held-out contexts. Each surface is exercised by the
synthetic benchmark below; real-data graduation remains gated by the
project claim ledger.

\subsection{Held-out gene panel recovery (synthetic placeholder)}
\label{sec:res-heldout}

We score each adapter on the held-out marker panel
$\{\textit{CD4}, \textit{CD8A}, \textit{CD68}, \textit{EPCAM},
\textit{ENG}, \textit{POSTN}\}$ defined statically before any imputation
runs (panel registered in \texttt{lumina\_st.benchmarks.panels.TME\_IMMUNE\_STROMAL}).

the nominal target within the regression band asserted by

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textsc{LuminaST} in its present synthetic-evaluation form is not a claim of
biological superiority over any prior method. The audit boundary requires
that every promotion of a claim from \texttt{validated-small} or
\texttt{blocked} to \texttt{paper-ready} be backed by real-data evidence
linked from \texttt{docs/CLAIM\_LEDGER.md}. The deliverables present today
are: (i)~a uniform benchmark contract that catches audit regressions
mechanically; (ii)~first-party split-conformal calibration that produces
prediction intervals at user-specified $\alpha$; (iii)~a per-transcript
Binomial thinning sweep that matches the physical noise model.

\subsection{Limitations}

Three limitations of the present manuscript are explicit. First, the
synthetic-data placeholder used for the held-out gene panel does not
exercise the compositional imbalance found in real cancer cohorts (e.g.,
the long-tail distribution of rare cell types in COAD vs OV vs CESC
slides), so reported recovery numbers should not be read as transfer
guarantees. Second, the conformal calibration produces coverage
guarantees that are conditional on exchangeability between calibration
and test residuals; that exchangeability has been verified for the
synthetic noise model but graduates to \texttt{paper-ready} only after
the Day~3 real-data evidence arrives via
\texttt{docs/DATA\_PREP\_CALENDAR.md}. Third, sub-cluster discovery and
DEG validation helpers ship in \texttt{lumina\_st.benchmarks.subpopulation}
but are demonstrated only on synthetic input; the paired-CODEX validation
described in stPainter \S2.4 is not in scope as the calendar does not
include a paired-protein dataset.

\subsection{Future work}

The immediate next milestone is the calendar Day~3 cervix Xenium subset
(see \texttt{docs/DATA\_PREP\_CALENDAR.md}), at which point the
held-out gene panel recovery moves from synthetic to real evidence and
the Sankey cluster-mapping composer skeleton at
\texttt{lumina-st/manuscript/compose\_sankey.py} can be re-driven against
real cluster identities. Subsequent calendar days unlock the
foundation-model latent encoder benchmark (Day~4), cross-cancer
leave-one-context cross-validation (Day~5), and the patch-based
concordance metric on real coordinates (Day~6). Each transition is
gated by the claim-ledger update described above; no claim graduates
without linked evidence.
